Four information orders for the visitor drawer

Same visitor, same tokens, same content: the verdict, the money, the ranked reasons, the journey with the block marker, the exclusion-list state, and one primary action. Four different orders. Every option keeps the rules: one primary button, the accent only for "you are here", red and amber only as alarms, mono numbers. The question each option answers first is the difference.

Option A

Verdict first, ledger last

Answer the question in the first line, then prove it below. The sentence is the a-ha; the money says what it cost; the reasons and the journey are the receipt for anyone who wants to check.

Reads best whenthe advertiser wants to know why in one glance and only sometimes digs.

Weaknessa skeptic scrolls a long way to the proof; the block marker is at the bottom of a 6-visit list.

Option B

Ledger first, verdict as a sticky bar

Show what happened and let the verdict arrive. The journey opens the drawer with the block marker in view; the verdict sentence sits in a slim bar that stays put while you scroll. Closest reading of "look at their journey and understand why".

Reads best whenthe advertiser distrusts summaries and wants to see the clicks with their own eyes.

Weaknessthe reasons and the money come after the journey; a skimmer sees visits, not a decision. Six visits fit; twelve push everything else off screen.

Option C

Tabs: Why, Journey, Actions

Separate the jobs into short panes so nothing scrolls. Why is the default; Journey is one tab over; the decision lives on its own tab with the sync state and the override history.

Reads best whenjourneys are long (20+ visits) and the drawer would otherwise become a wall.

Weaknessthe proof is one click away from the claim, which is the trust problem restated. Hover-linking a reason to its visits stops working across a tab boundary.

Option D

Two panes: reasons beside the journey

Claim and evidence visible at once. The reasons and the money sit on the left; the journey runs down the right; hovering a reason lights its visits without anything scrolling out of view. The drawer widens to make room.

Reads best whenthe user is auditing, comparing what the system said against what the visits show.

Weaknessneeds a 640px drawer, which leaves a 13-inch laptop with a sliver of table, undoing the reason the drawer exists. Cramped below 1280px.

How to choose

A optimises for the first line. B optimises for seeing the clicks. C optimises for short panes at the cost of the claim-to-proof link. D optimises for auditing at the cost of the table.

The brief's test is one sentence: a customer opens one blocked visitor, looks at their journey, and understands why. A and B both pass it; they disagree about which of "why" and "journey" a person needs first. C and D each trade away one of the two things the drawer is for.

Mixes worth considering: A with B's sticky verdict bar (the sentence stays visible while scrolling the ledger), or B with the money folded into the sticky bar.
