Two hierarchies for the visitor detail

The built page makes the reader assemble the explanation from two dense columns. Both options below answer the same five questions in order: what was decided, what pattern caused it, what changed on the visit that triggered it, what supports or weakens the conclusion, and what happened after. Both use golden case 7 (190.2.145.19, blocked after visit 5, moderate confidence, Meta Ads sync failed) and copy that the data could produce. Option A is the recommended design. Option B is the agent's alternative, built to cover what A leaves open.

A · recommended

Two columns, rebalanced: journey beside a short evidence summary

The verdict, its explanation and why confidence is moderate lead. A compact facts row and a compact enforcement notice follow. The journey (65%) sits beside a sticky evidence summary (35%) grouped into primary reasons, what tipped the decision and what weakened it. Audit detail is behind one "View all evidence" disclosure and per-visit disclosures. On narrow screens the columns stack: summary, evidence, journey, enforcement, raw evidence.

Solves
  • Reads top to bottom as a decision, not a ledger
  • Explanation and journey stay side by side, so a reason can be checked against the visits without leaving the page
  • Confidence is explained, not just labelled
  • The block marker becomes the strongest element in the journey
Leaves open
  • Two disclosure systems for the same evidence: "View all evidence" and "View visit evidence" on each visit
  • Question 5, what happened after, is answered only by reading the tail of the timeline
  • The frame is written for Blocked; Monitoring and Not blocked need their own copy for "tipped" and "weakened"
  • The narrow order reflows, so the explanation and the journey relate differently on a phone

B · agent's alternative

Explanation first, one reading order: the evidence lives inside the decision

Same four levels, but Level 2 (primary reasons, what tipped it, what weakened it) sits inside the decision block as a three-part strip that reuses the table's signal labels, so the row and the page share one vocabulary. An "After the decision" strip answers question 5 directly: enforcement per platform, returns, paid clicks since. The journey runs full width beneath, split by the block marker into before and after. One "Audit trail" disclosure at the end holds every raw value. The order is identical at every width, so nothing reflows on a phone.

Solves
  • One reading order top to bottom, the same on a phone, so the relation between explanation and journey never changes
  • Question 5 has its own answer instead of a timeline tail; criteria 5 and 7 (what cost money, did the block work) are visible without scrolling the journey
  • One disclosure for audit detail instead of "View all evidence" plus a link on every visit
  • The frame holds for every status: the strip's headings become "What we are watching" and "Why it has not blocked" for Monitoring, and "Nothing crossed the line" for Not blocked
  • Continuity with the table: the same signal labels the row showed appear first in the strip
Costs
  • A longer page; the journey starts below the fold on a laptop
  • Evidence and visits are not side by side, so a reason is checked against the journey by anchor, not by glance
  • The three-part strip gets tight between about 900 and 1000px and stacks below that

How to choose

Both options keep the same four levels and the same strengthened elements: the verdict line, the two primary reasons, what tipped the decision, the block marker and the reason confidence is moderate. They differ in where Level 2 lives and how the page behaves when it narrows.

  • Choose A if checking a reason against the visits at a glance matters more than a single reading order. Its cost is two disclosure systems and a reflow on phones.
  • Choose B if the page must read the same way on every screen and answer "what happened after" without scanning the timeline. Its cost is a longer page with evidence and visits apart.
  • A hybrid is cheap: A's two columns with B's strip inside the decision block and B's "After the decision" panel, leaving the right column for the journey's own summary. Say so if that is the shape.

What the data cannot say yet, and will need in the build: a reason for the confidence label (which signals were present or absent), a fourth evidence kind for the tipping event, a failure reason on sync events, and per-visit "changed since the previous visit" facts. The canvas writes these by hand.
